## What was built

After the reload (S09-T02) the destination world is **held**: the barrier
stays up — each player's input for the tick is consumed and discarded (so
the player still counts as in the world) and every action but the ready
is refused with reason 30 — until every bound player's client has sent a
**ready** (ActionIntent 13: the transition id and the level its world is
live on). The server accepts a ready only for the held transition and its
level (31 otherwise) and only once that client's world is live on the
server's side (32 otherwise; the client re-sends 2 s later); a repeat
returns the stored outcome. All ready → `travel-resumed why=all-ready`
and CampaignEvent 13 state 9 to both; the **deadline is 60 s** after the
reload (`why=timeout`, abort field 9): the present players resume, the
absent one rejoins later as a detached player would (decision T03-2). The
committed event now carries the destination generation. A client cannot
act against the old level: it left that world on the committed event
(replica and prediction reset), its input queue restarts at the join, and
the hold discards input until the resume.

Test knobs (client profile): `travel_ready_delay` (seconds after arriving
until the ready; 999 never), `travel_ready_wrong` (a ready with a wrong
transition id, then one with a wrong level, then the right one).

## Limits found here

- A duplicated ready (packet duplication) was not exercised by a run: the
  stored-outcome path is the same code every travel kind uses (tv-3: 18
  duplicates) but no ready was repeated here (`ready_duplicates=0`).
- During the hold the world's NPCs and clock run (decision T03-1); a
  player standing at the arrival point for up to 60 s is exposed.
- The 60-s deadline is a stated policy, not tuned (S09-T04 owns the
  failure matrix).
