## What was built

The failure matrix of a travel is now defined (contract §11) and every
window in it is either exercised by a run below or carried from S09-T01…T03.
The code added for it:

- **Who broke the barrier.** A party member's disconnect or death during
  the barrier already rolled the travel back; the remaining player is now
  told the reason (requester/partner left or dead, as a live proposal's
  abort names it) instead of a bare execution failure (decision T04-1).
- **The held notice** (CampaignEvent 13 state 10). A client whose world
  goes live on the destination during the hold without having been told
  the transition (its first process died at the load and the harness
  restarted it, or it reconnected) is told the transition id, the level and
  the time left, and reports ready like the others. Before, such a client
  would have waited for the deadline without knowing why (T04-5).
- **The hold is over.** A ready that names the client's own held transition
  and its own level but meets no hold on the server (the hold timed out
  before the client came back, or the server was restarted and holds
  nothing) is refused 31 as before, and the client now reads that refusal
  as "the party went on": it stops waiting and plays (`travel-hold-over`).
  The server logs it apart from a genuinely wrong ready
  (`travel-ready-no-hold`). A new authority epoch at the Welcome (a
  restarted server) voids any hold or arrival the client was waiting for
  (`travel-hold-void`, T04-4).
- **Crash windows.** Failpoints `travel:reloading` (the sessions detached,
  the level being replaced) and `travel:after-ready` (the hold, one player
  ready) beside S09-T02's `travel:before-take` and `travel:after-commit`.
- **Harness.** `travel_barrier_hold` (server) keeps the barrier up for N s
  so a disconnect can land in it (the barrier otherwise drains in 0 ticks);
  the step driver's connection drop (flags bit 2) on a travel answer waits
  for the answer's result, so the drop lands in the barrier and not before
  the consent; `travel_rejoin_delay` (client) delays the rejoin after a
  committed travel; `travel_load_fail` (client) terminates the process when
  the destination world is received (a crash during the load). Fixture
  variants 3 (accept-then-drop, then a retry) and 4 (two travels; the
  first's publication failed from outside with `checkpoint:after-manifest:3:fail`).

## Limits found here

- A **death during the barrier** takes the same branch as a disconnect
  (tv-17) but was not exercised by a run.
- A **failed take** (no entity for a party actor) shares `travel_exec_fail`
  with the publication failure (tv-18) but cannot be injected.
- The restarted server after a post-commit crash **holds nothing**
  (decision T04-4): each player plays as soon as it rejoins (tv-19, tv-20).
- The server notices a dead client's connection after the **liveness
  timeout (~15–22 s)**; a client that crashed and restarts faster than that
  replaces its own session (`replaced: a new connection of the same
  player`, tv-25) and is served at once.
- The build identity hashes the engine sources: both products must be
  rebuilt together (tv-24).
- The fixture's changer choice depends on the engine's list order (tv-23).
