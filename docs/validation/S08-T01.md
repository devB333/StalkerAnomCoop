## Deliverable

- **Wire (v1.6, `protocol/`):** action kind 5 `pickup` (target = the item,
  no params); named refusal reasons (`ActionReason`: 8 ItemGone, 9
  ItemTaken, 10 OutOfReach, 11 NotTakeable, plus the earlier 1–7); lifecycle
  message `Reparent` 0x0023 (entity, new parent or none, transform, commit
  id); journal kind `kRecordPickup` 7.
- **Server (`CoopServerHost.cpp`, `take_pickup`)**, the six phases of the
  contract:
  1. *Receive:* a known operation id returns its stored outcome and commit.
  2. *Validate* on the server's state at that tick: attached session; the
     player's own participant actor alive; the target resolves to a current
     published object (`engine_id_of`: the ref **with its generation**); an
     online inventory item (`CSE_ALifeInventoryItem` + `CInventoryItem`);
     no owner on the server entity or the level object, and not in the
     confirm queue of a pickup applied this tick; one the engine lets an
     actor take (`CanTake`, not a rocket, a useful missile,
     `CInventory::CanTakeItem`); within 2 m + 0.5 m slack of the actor's
     feet-to-eyes axis (the engine's own reach: `CActor::UpdateCL` pick
     `RQ.range < 2`, `PickupModeUpdate_COD` 2 m).
  3. *Resolve* absolute post-state: owner entity + item entity, section,
     position and the item's native spawn state (`Spawn_Write`).
  4. *Commit* the `pickup` record (write-through journal) before anything
     is applied or acknowledged; a failed store fails closed and stops the
     server (`StoreFailed`).
  5. *Apply* through the engine's own ownership path: the bridge's
     `GE_OWNERSHIP_TAKE` (with `GE_OWNERSHIP_REJECT` first from any other
     parent). `xrServer::Process_event_ownership` sets the parent
     synchronously, so a second claim later in the same tick sees the owner
     and is refused ItemTaken. The level actor's take is then confirmed
     (`pickup-confirmed`, ≤ 5 s, else `unconfirmed`/`reverted`).
  6. *Acknowledge* Accepted with the commit revision; remember the outcome.
- **Replication:** `track_lifecycle` emits `Reparent` for **every** parent
  change of a published entity (also NPC pickups and drops, the K4 class),
  after the tick's spawns, in commit order.
- **Recovery:** `pickup` records after `R_G` are replayed by the same
  adapter (idempotent, absolute post-state; re-creation from the recorded
  spawn state under the recorded EntityId when the checkpoint lacks the
  item; deferral while the owner's level object is missing) and their
  outcomes re-remembered, so a retried claim gets the original answer.
- **Client (`CoopClientLink.cpp`, `game_cl_base.cpp`):** every pickup path
  ends in `game_cl_GameState::SendPickUpEvent`; on a co-op client it becomes
  the pickup intent naming the replica's ref, and the local ownership event
  is never sent. Unanswered intents are re-sent every 3 s with the same id
  (across reconnects). `Reparent` is applied through REJECT/TAKE on the
  client's in-process server under `ReplicaPermit`. A refused player
  pickup shows "Someone else took it." / "Too far to pick up." / "You can't
  pick that up." through `actor_menu.set_msg`.
- **Race fixture:** server profile `pickup_race` (start delay) and
  `pickup_rounds`: every 8 s an item spawns midway between the two actors
  and both clients are told (CampaignEvent kind 6) the server tick at which
  to claim it; then three refusal rounds (an item 8 m away, the other
  player's actor, a nonexistent target). Client script 7 claims at the
  named tick, repeats each claim with the same id, and after losing once
  with a new id. In each race round one client (round % 2 = its slot)
  claims through the **real use key** instead of a direct intent: it aims
  its camera at the item and presses and holds USE as a player does
  (`CActor::IR_OnKeyboardPress(kUSE)`, and every frame while held what the
  held key does under `g_multi_item_pickup`: pickup mode, `Actor.cpp`
  1134-1142, via the new `CActor::coop_hold_use_key`); the engine's own
  pickup (`CActor::PickupModeUpdate` / `PickupModeUpdate_COD`,
  `Actor_Feel.cpp`) then calls `SendPickUpEvent`, which becomes that
  round's intent (`key-linked`). The race item spawns 30 % of the way from
  that client (within the crosshair pick's 2 m from the eye; both claims
  stay within the server's reach). Failpoints `pickup:before-append|after-flush|after-apply|after-ack`.
- **Tools:** `prepare-coop-session.py --pickup c1,c2 [--pickup-rounds 8]
  [--pickup-start 20]`; `tools/compare-coop-pickup.py` (eleven checks listed
  in its docstring, `use_key` among them; `--expect-restart` adds
  `restart_recovered`).

### pick-1 (campaign-246 / client-148): every claim refused "item gone"

All 22 claims (8 race rounds × 2 clients, plus the far item) were refused
reason 8 in ~16 ms; no commit. Cause: an item's identity generation turns
when ALife switches it online (`CampaignIdentity.cpp:186`, the S03-T01
fixture's "the switch still turns the generation"). The fixture announced
the round right after `spawn_item`, while the item was still offline
(`target=26348/1`); by claim time the item was online and published as
generation 2, so the server correctly refused the stale ref. The other two
refusal rounds already behaved: the other actor → 11, a nonexistent target
→ 8, on both clients. **Fix (fixture only):** a spawned round item waits
until its Spawn has been sent to every in-world client (`sent_commit ≥` its
publishing commit, up to 10 s, else `unpublished`) and the round then names
the published ref. Also fixed in the comparison tool: the world csv's
leading `#` comment line, and the arrival-tick measure (each slot's first
claim, not the repeats).

### The use-key path: pick-8 (aborted), pick-11, pick-12

The crash matrix and pick-3 claimed through direct intents only, so the
player's own path (use key → engine pickup → `SendPickUpEvent` → intent)
had not run unattended. The driver above was added in three steps:

- **pick-8 (campaign-248 / client-150, aborted):** the key client aimed at
  the item and sent a synthetic USE press every 200 ms; nothing was picked
  (`pickup-key-missed … looking_at=-1`), every key round fell back to a
  direct intent. Stopped early; no logs kept.
- **pick-11 (campaign-249 / client-151):** the item moved to 30 % of the way
  from the key client and the miss now logs the crosshair pick: `pick=-1
  pick_range=1.75 eye_distance=1.73` — the pick works but nothing in
  pickup mode used it. Cause: the clients run with `g_multi_item_pickup
  on`, under which pickup mode is set only while the USE key is physically
  held (`Actor.cpp` 1134-1142 polls `iGetAsyncKeyState`) and cleared at the
  end of every `UpdateCL` (1294); a key event does not set it
  (`ActorInput.cpp` 537 only without multi-pickup). The other ten checks
  passed; `use_key` failed (0 linked).
- **pick-12 (campaign-250 / client-152):** the driver holds the key
  (`coop_hold_use_key` each frame while held). **All eleven checks pass**:
  every key round linked a `SendPickUpEvent` request 3–17 ms after the
  claim tick (`use_key`: c1 4/4, c2 4/4, no fallback); 8 race rounds, one
  winner each (c1 ×5, c2 ×3; the key client won 5 of 8), 6 in the same
  server tick and all 8 within 2 ticks; 8 commits, all held, both clients
  agree, all confirmed; the refusal rounds as in pick-3.

## Carried limits touched

- **L01** (only movement reaches the server): narrowed — item pickup now
  reaches the server as a validated, durable action, from the use key and
  every other pickup path (`SendPickUpEvent`). Inventory moves, drops,
  trade, use and dialogue remain.
- **L36** (inventory/quest/reward effects not durable): narrowed — pickups
  are durable journal records with an idempotent adapter and crash tests.
  Drops, transfers, consumption, trade, quest and reward effects remain
  (S08-T02…T06).
- **K4** (a client keeps an NPC's items): narrowed — every owner change is
  replicated (seen once: an NPC's drop, pick-13). An NPC take near the
  players and an NPC going offline with items were not observed.

## Limits (new)

- **Consumption and drops after a pickup are not durable.** The pickup
  record is absolute post-state: after a restart the item is back in the
  owner's inventory even if it was used or dropped after the pickup
  (those effects roll back with the checkpoint, as the contract allows for
  non-E effects; S08-T02 gives drops/transfers records).
- **The installed scripts' item callbacks run where the engine runs them.**
  The client's `actor_on_item_before_pickup` (`bind_stalker_ext`) can veto a
  pickup locally before the intent is sent; the server's take runs the
  server actor's take callbacks; neither is a durable effect, and their
  script side effects on pickup (if any, per item) are not audited.
- **One host, loopback.** The races are between two clients on one PC
  (as every S05–S07 session); arrival order over the Internet is the
  server's arrival order, which is the rule, but it is not measured there.
- **The NoActor (3) refusal for a dead player's claim was not exercised.**
