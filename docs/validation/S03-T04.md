## What the engine does (source)

- The ALife switch rules measure every object's distance from the one ALife
  actor, `CALifeGraphRegistry::actor()` (the `M_SPAWN_OBJECT_ASPLAYER` entity,
  `alife_graph_registry.cpp:53-57`): `CSE_ALifeDynamicObject::try_switch_online`
  brings an object online when the actor is within `online_distance`
  (`alife_dynamic_object.cpp:160`) and `try_switch_offline` takes it offline
  beyond `offline_distance` (`:180`); the online-offline groups (the squads)
  use their members' positions (`alife_online_offline_group.cpp:226,263`), the
  monster groups too (`alife_group_abstract.cpp:164`). Squad members are not
  in the level registry while their squad handles them
  (`alife_online_offline_group.cpp:102-130`). The distances come from
  `alife.ltx`: `switch_distance = 750`, `switch_factor = 0.1`, so online 675 m
  and offline 825 m on k00_marsh (`alife_switch_manager_inline.h:36-40`). The
  loop is `CALifeUpdateManager::update_switch` (`alife_update_manager.cpp:92-100`),
  run from `update()` on the campaign tick thread (`CampaignServerClock.cpp:151`
  runs `seqParallel`).
- The actor's server entity position the rule reads is updated from the
  actor's own `net_Export` (`Actor_Network.cpp:93-101`,
  `xrServer_process_update.cpp:41`); every participant actor on the server is
  local and relevant (S03-T03), so both entities follow their actors (observed:
  0.00 m gap within 9–12 ticks of a teleport, below).
- Awareness: a monster's enemies come from its hit, sound and visual memory
  (`monster_enemy_memory.cpp:49-110`, generic) plus two rules about "the
  player": a remembered dangerous sound while the player watches the monster,
  and the player within feel distance (50 m for the boar) while the player
  watches it. Both ran against `g_actor`. `CEnemyManager::useful` gates every
  enemy (alive, visible for AI, relation, a valid level vertex, then the
  scripted enemy callback, `enemy_manager.cpp:76-116`);
  `CBaseMonster::feel_vision_isRelevant` also needs a different team and an
  awake monster (`base_monster_feel.cpp:222-245`). Stalker enemy selection is
  generic; `LookAtActor` tested `Actor()` after choosing the nearest
  participant (`ai_stalker.cpp:1679,1694`).
- AI vision on the headless server: the frustum query, ray traces and memory
  run as on the client (`CustomMonster.cpp:599-718`, `vision_client.cpp:41-118`,
  `feel_vision.cpp:114-180`), but the visibility value is scaled by the
  target's luminosity, `ROS()->get_luminocity()`
  (`visual_memory_manager.cpp:322-328`), which only the renderer's lighting
  pass fills; the server never renders, so it stays at the constructor's
  `approximate = (0,0,0)` (`Layers/xrRender/LightTrack.cpp:23`). The engine
  clamps it to 0.001 and raises it to the viewer's `luminocity_factor`
  (boar 0.6 → 0.016, actor 0.5 → 0.032, `m_boar.ltx:304`, `actor.ltx:123`);
  the installed `visual_memory_manager.get_visible_value` keeps that factor in
  its formula. Observed: `luminocity=0.000` for every target in smoke-123..127.
  AI on the server therefore sees as in near darkness; see Limits.
- An actor's vision looks along its active camera (`CActorMemory::camera`,
  `actor_memory.cpp:38-52`); `cam_Update` runs for every actor
  (`Actor.cpp:1189`), so each participant's camera sits at its eye (observed
  1.7 m above the actor) and keeps the yaw it was last given; nothing on the
  server turns it (`CScriptGameObject::SetActorDirection` sets
  `cam_Active()->Set(yaw, …)`, `script_game_object2.cpp:411-421`).

## Fixture

Runs after `campaign-two-actors-passed`, which leaves participant 2 holding a
live actor 3 m beside actor 1. Per tick it records the level registry's
online states (every switch with its distances to both actors), the anchors,
and the frame counters. Phases:

1. **baseline** — settle (no switch for 50 ticks); every modelled object
   satisfies the switch rule against the switch manager's anchors (offline and
   allowed online within `online − 2 m`, or online and allowed offline beyond
   `offline + 2 m`, is a violation; squads by their nearest member; the 2 m
   tolerance covers objects moving between two passes); the anchors are the two
   actors; 250 ticks measured; the distance rule timed over the registry.
2. **separate** — actor 2 is teleported as `SetActorPosition` does to the
   farthest accessible vertex from actor 1 (breadth-first over the whole AI
   map); settle; the rule holds; every switchable object near actor 2 and
   beyond actor 1's offline distance is online; objects offline at the
   baseline came online for actor 2 alone; nothing online near actor 1 went
   offline; 250 ticks measured. If the level could not separate the actors by
   the offline distance + 100 m the switch distance would be lowered for the
   fixture only; k00_marsh did not need it.
3. **awareness** — god mode on; a `boar_normal` is spawned about 6 m from each
   actor (in front of its camera where an accessible vertex is there, else
   beside it), each actor is faced to its boar as `SetActorDirection` does;
   both boars come online (the second only through actor 2's anchor, being
   beyond actor 1's offline distance) and each takes its own actor as its
   enemy (`EnemyMan.get_enemy()`) within 750 ticks; both are released. A
   per-150-tick report logs every gate of the enemy and vision pipeline
   (`aware` rows).
4. **disconnect** — participant 2's session detaches (`detach_session`); for
   150 ticks the retained actor stays an anchor, the objects that came online
   for it stay online, the level, server, ALife clock and frame driver go on;
   the session re-attaches to the same actor.
5. **return** — actor 2 back beside actor 1; settle; the rule holds; every
   object that came online for actor 2 alone (still beyond both actors'
   offline distance) went offline.

## Acceptance

- **Entities needed near either player remain online**: met. With the actors
  1,098 m apart the switch rule held for all 1,061 objects, every switchable
  object near actor 2 was online, nothing near actor 1 went offline, and the
  far area went offline again on return (smoke-128, 129).
- **An NPC can detect/target either**: met for the participant rules — a boar
  took each actor as its enemy, through the converted "a participant watches
  me" rule, actor 2's boar online only through actor 2's anchor. Bounded by
  the server's AI vision (Limits).
- **One disconnect does not destroy the level context**: met. A detached
  participant's retained actor keeps its area online; level, server, ALife and
  clock go on; the session re-attaches (actor release on disconnect is
  S04-T05).
- Tick cost measured (table above); player-bound abilities recorded.

## Limits

- **Server AI vision runs in darkness (open item, not a participant effect).**
  Luminosity is 0 on the headless server, so monsters' and stalkers'
  visibility builds 30–60× slower than at full light (the factor depends on
  the viewer; the client's actual light levels were not measured). The boars
  never saw an actor by their own vision in seven runs; detection worked
  through the actor-watches rule and would also work through hit and sound
  memory. This affects all server-side AI (stalker vs stalker included) since
  Stage 2 and needs a server-side light model (sun/hemisphere from the
  weather and time, sky occlusion from the collision model; no renderer
  lights). Proposed as a new task; not a placeholder constant.
- A server-driven participant's camera keeps its last yaw; the fixture turns
  it as `SetActorDirection` does. Real clients send their view (Stage 4).
- Only the boar was exercised for awareness; stalker enemy selection is
  generic and was not separately run against actor 2. The awareness guards
  for poltergeist, bloodsucker and the other abilities are compile-checked
  only.
- `campaign-hearing-failed` (exit 16) recurred in 3 of 13 GameState runs
  (smoke-120, 122, 131). Candidate from the config: `[stalker_step_manager]`
  (`configs/creatures/m_stalker.ltx:6-66`) lists every walk direction for
  index `_0` but only `norm_walk_fwd_1` for index `_1`; a walk in an `_1`
  motion in any other direction has no step parameters, and
  `CStepManager::on_animation_start` disables steps for it. Next check: log
  the stalker's legs motion name during the walk. Not an identity or
  activation effect.
- The detach phase does not release the actor; release policy is S04-T05.
- Tick wall time is paced by the campaign clock, so the cost is read from the
  frame's object-update time and the update counters, not from wall time.
