## Question

Plan.md S06-T01: sequence input batches per player; bound time windows and
queue sizes; validate identity, movement modes, speed/stamina and collision;
drive the native controller on the server; reject teleport and outcome
packets. **Acceptance:** server movement obeys collision and constraints;
duplicate or stale inputs cannot double-advance; spoofed outcomes cannot move
actors. **Validation:** two actors moving against obstacles; malformed,
time-shifted and impossible-speed input tests. **Risk:** native movement may
depend on camera orientation or client timestep; translate intention
explicitly.

## What was built

- **Intent only.** A client sends movement axes, stance, the walk button and
  the camera's yaw and pitch, numbered by input tick. No client message
  carries a position, speed or outcome. A `Snapshot` from a client is a
  wrong-direction violation that closes the connection. Every `ActionIntent`
  is refused, since no action kind is defined.
- **Sequencing and bounds** (`protocol/include/coop/proto/input_queue.h`, new).
  There is one `InputQueue` per player. An input is refused as *invalid* when
  a field is non-finite, an axis is outside −1..1, the pitch exceeds 1.6, the
  stance is Prone (the actor has no prone mode) or a button is unknown. It is
  refused as *stale* when its tick is at or before the last consumed tick, or
  is already queued, and as *ahead* when it exceeds the newest accepted tick
  by more than 250. Otherwise it is queued in tick order, with at most 5
  waiting and the oldest dropped. The server consumes **exactly one input per
  tick**. When the queue is empty it reuses the last input for at most 10
  ticks, then applies none. An input therefore moves the actor for at most
  one tick, and a flood or backlog adds at most 100 ms of latency, never
  distance.
- **Client timestep removed.** The client samples once per server tick of
  wall time, at the Welcome's `tick_rate_hz` (50 Hz), and restarts its tick
  count on every connection. A frame longer than three ticks does not produce
  a burst. On the server the native control path runs with the server's own
  timestep: `CActor::shedule_Update` → `g_cl_CheckControls` (`CanMove`,
  crouch box, `CanSprint`/`CanAccelerate`, the speed factors, stamina through
  `DisableSprint`) → `g_cl_Orientate` → `g_Physics`. The camera's influence is
  the yaw and pitch the player chose; the pitch is clamped to the camera's own
  `lim_pitch` (`CoopServerHost.cpp`, `apply_inputs`).
- **Identity.** Input is applied only to the actor the session's context
  resolves (`Scope::Self`). A detached player's queue is reset. Snapshots
  acknowledge the last consumed tick (`last_input`).
- **Defect fixed: two actors pinned each other** (`Actor.cpp`,
  `CActor::UpdateCL`). Each actor runs `UpdateObjectBox` on every character
  in its touch range. That function fits an NPC's box to the player's camera
  and recalculates the NPC's movement with **zero acceleration**
  (`PHMovementControl.cpp`, `CPHMovementControl::UpdateObjectBox`). An NPC's
  own movement manager sets its acceleration again; another actor has no such
  manager. Within about 1.5 m, each actor therefore zeroed the other's control
  acceleration after its control step and before the physics step. Neither
  could move in any direction again. The actor now skips other actors there.
  A single-player world has one actor, so single-player is unchanged. S05-T03
  runs view-4 and view-5 recorded "walked into actor 2 at 2 m (collision)";
  that was most likely this pin.
- **Diagnostics.** `CActor::control_trace` records what the last native
  control and physics step did: input bits, `CanMove`, the environment, the
  real state, the acceleration, the physics body's flags and its velocity.
  The server logs it after each tick that applied an input, with the living
  creatures within 3 m.
- **Test driver** (profile `script = 2`, `CoopClientLink.cpp`
  `run_script2`; `prepare-coop-session.py --movement`): legs A–K, described
  below. `tools/compare-coop-movement.py` (new) measures them from the
  server's positions, logged at every snapshot, and its input log.

What this shows, against the acceptance criteria:
- **Duplicates and stale input cannot double-advance.** Batches sent four
  times, a counter advancing seven ticks per sample and a full replay all
  produced the same speed as the normal stream, or no movement at all.
- **Spoofed outcomes cannot move actors.** A teleport intent and a forged
  position were refused. Neither changed the actor's position, and the forged
  position ended the connection.
- **Constraints and collision come from the native controller.** Crouch ran
  at about 45 % of walking speed and sprint at about 2× (the installed speed
  factors). An unsupported stance did nothing. The two actors' bodies never
  overlapped: the closest approach was 0.52 m, with 0.30–0.35 m boxes. A
  sprinting actor glances off and slides past, as a player slides past an NPC.

**Rule changes made after seeing data**, recorded so the evidence can be
judged:
1. The B and C bounds were a ±25 % displacement ratio against A. In move-2 a
   slope shortened a leg at normal peak speed. The bound became a speed limit:
   peak at most 1.15× A's, and distance no more than that speed allows over
   the leg.
2. "Still" legs (D, G, J, K) required at most 0.15 m. Physics settling after
   a previous leg reached 0.17 m. They now require that the server applied no
   movement input in the leg, from its own log, and at most 0.25 m.
3. The sprint rule expected a head-on stop. Once the actors could move, the
   sprinter glanced off. It now requires no overlap, a push of at most 1.0 m
   (the earlier 0.5 m was a guess with no reference) and a sprint faster than A.

move-8 was run after the final rules were written.

## Limits

- **Stamina was not exhausted in a test.** Sprint and stamina go through the
  native `CanSprint`/`DisableSprint` path, and power went from 1.000 to 0.999.
  A long sprint until stamina refuses it was not run.
- **The pitch clamp and the 10-tick hold were not exercised** by a dedicated
  co-op leg; the hold is covered by unit tests. Jump, lean, climb (ladders)
  and vehicles are not in the input model.
- **Static obstacles were not asserted per leg.** Terrain slowed legs as the
  native controller does (move-2). The actors' collision with the level is the
  native one shown in G02 (S02-T03d and S02-T04b). Collision between the two
  actors is tested here, but how far one actor pushes another has no
  single-player reference.
- **One input per tick means a client that falls behind loses ticks.** The
  backlog drops its oldest input: 0.2–1.3 % per player across move-6..8. A
  client that runs ahead is clipped to 5 waiting inputs. Prediction
  (S06-T03) will need the server's acknowledged tick for each correction.
- `UpdateObjectBox` still reads `Device.vCameraDirection` for NPC boxes near
  an actor. On the server there is no camera; this is pre-existing and
  unchanged for NPCs.
- One host, loopback, two clients, short sessions (under 4 minutes).
