# S06-T02 — Snapshots, interpolation and baselines

**Status: Completed (2026-10-21).** Engine `c57fe5b9` + patches 0001–0032;
protocol v1.2 (`proto-build-26`). Design:
[co-op session](../coop-session.md#snapshots-interpolation-and-baselines-s06-t02),
[wire format](../network-protocol.md#wire-format-v1).

## What changed

**Protocol v1.2** (workspace files, built by `tools/Build-Protocol.ps1`):
- `Snapshot` has its own `snapshot_id` (its parts share id, baseline, tick and
  last input) and two new components: `kRotation` (a unit quaternion) and
  `kRemoved` (no fields). `SnapshotAck` has a `missing_baseline` field, and
  its snapshot id may be 0. Both peers come from one build, so the layout
  change is not negotiated.
- `snapshot.h`: one `SnapshotSender` per connection on the server. It sends
  deltas against the newest snapshot the client acknowledged with every part
  decoded; an entry carries only the changed components and removals are
  explicit.
  - The sender stores the frame the client will hold, so a change that did not
    fit the part budget keeps its baseline state there and is sent later.
    Every decoded snapshot reproduces the sender's frame exactly.
  - History is 32 snapshots on the sender and 64 on the receiver. If the
    acknowledged baseline is evicted or the client reports a missing one, the
    next snapshot is full, inside the same part budget.
  - The part budget (8 at most) halves on congestion and grows back after 10
    clean snapshots. Deferred entities move ahead each snapshot; the
    participant actors are pinned first.
  - `SnapshotReceiver` assembles parts and acknowledges complete snapshots. It
    hands a state on only when it is newer than the last one handed on for
    that entity.
- `interpolation.h`:
  - `ServerClock`: a 2-s windowed minimum of arrival offsets, slewing at
    most 5 ms per observation.
  - `InterpolationBuffer`: interpolates; holds a still entity's previous state
    across a gap; extrapolates a moving creature along its velocity for at most
    500 ms, then glides back.

**Engine (patch 0032)**, 9 files: `CoopServerHost.cpp`, `CoopClientLink.cpp`,
`CoopConfig.{h,cpp}`, `CoopReplica.{h,cpp}`, `CustomMonster.{h,cpp}`,
`Level_network_spawn.cpp`.
- **Server snapshot set**, per player:
  - the participant actors;
  - every other online creature, nearest first;
  - unparented physics objects while they move, or until the player has
    acknowledged their resting pose.

  It is capped at 256 entities and only includes entities whose Spawn has
  already been sent to that player. Each creature's velocity is its observed
  displacement between the server's own position changes, and zero once it
  has stopped (the controller velocity of a distant dog read 6× its real
  speed in snap-3). Until a player acknowledges its first snapshot it gets one
  every 500 ms (snap-1 sent 130 full snapshots during the checkpoint
  download).
- **Client**:
  - Decodes from the moment its checkpoint is in and acknowledges every
    complete snapshot.
  - Tracks every presented entity; a known entity without a track is seeded
    from the newest complete snapshot.
  - Presents actors through `CActor::replica_present`, NPCs through the new
    `CCustomMonster::replica_present`, crows by taking over their flight for
    the frame, and physics objects through their shell transform (put to
    sleep).
  - The server clock restarts at live.
- **NPCs stay remote on a replica (defect found here).**
  `CLevel::cl_Process_Spawn` forces every spawn local when the level hosts the
  in-process server, which undid the replica's remote flag. NPCs had therefore
  been running their AI on clients since S05-T02, idle because their script
  binders do not update. Once NPCs moved, one ran an installed script action
  on client 2 in session-4 and crashed it (`death_manager.script:682`, from
  `CAI_Stalker::Think`).
  - Fix: `replica_keeps_remote` keeps non-actor creatures remote on a replica.
  - Remote creatures get the update queue at spawn.
  - `CCustomMonster::UpdateCL` now selects animations for a remote creature.
    Without that, NPCs stood in the bind pose; the user saw Fanat, the
    companion NPC, T-posing.
  - The only creature still local on a client is the parked car (`m_car`, no
    AI).
- **Fixtures:**
  - profile `drop = N` (server): a medkit spawned and tossed beside player 1
    every N seconds (an item comes online already resting on the ground);
  - `net_loss` / `net_lag` (any process): the library's simulated impairment
    of what that process sends;
  - test-driver step: face the nearest NPC stalker, log its animation blend
    count, take a screenshot;
  - new tool `tools/compare-coop-snapshots.py`.
- **Test-driver fixes:** `other_actor()` returned the last track, which is no
  longer an actor now that tracks hold NPCs. The forged-snapshot leg needed a
  snapshot id: v1.2's encoder refused the forgery, so it was never sent.

**Rule changes made after seeing data**, recorded so the evidence can be
judged:
1. Crows (`m_crow`) are reported apart as "flyer" and are not in the checks.
   The server moves them in jumps of about 6 m every half second; a client
   shows them flying smoothly between those points (snap-4).
2. The reference for creatures is the line through the server's position
   changes. A hold shorter than 1 s is a scheduling step; a longer one is
   kept as a stop. The server moves a distant NPC only when it is scheduled,
   so its log is a step function, and a smooth client is off it by up to half
   a step: about 2 m for a running dog. With the raw step reference, moving
   p95 was 0.34–0.56 m across snap-5…9. The raw figure is still reported.
3. Speed over ±0.4 s instead of between consecutive rows, for the same
   reason.

The bounds themselves (p95 ≤ 0.5 m, coverage 95 % / 90 %, rest 5 cm) were
not changed.

## Acceptance

- **A missing baseline triggers a bounded full resync:** unit tests for
  eviction and for a client-reported missing baseline. In the sessions there
  were 3–4 lost baselines per player, all at the join, every full snapshot
  within the part budget, and 0 missing baselines on any client, including
  under loss.
- **Old snapshots never overwrite newer state:** the receiver hands on only
  newer states (unit tests with reordering and duplicates; 0 order errors in
  2,000 random snapshots), and the transport guard drops older transient
  sequences first (client `snap_older` = 0).
- **Reliable events are not blocked by obsolete motion:** the wire scenario
  (lifecycle p50 unchanged under a snapshot flood beyond the link rate) and the
  separate transient lane. In the sessions, churn (session-6) and lifecycle
  stayed exact.
- **Bandwidth measured:** 10.8–14.1 KB/s per client steady, about 60 KB/s for
  a few seconds at a join.

## Limits

- **Other actor's equipment and pose:** a participant actor's active item is
  not replicated, so player 2's actor seen by player 1 shows no weapon. Its
  stance on screenshots is still unexplained (it looked like the bind pose).
  Owner: S06-T04 (the weapon slice equips and shows weapons).
- **NPC animation:** NPCs animate from their own remote state (idle or stand).
  Movement type, body state and aim are not replicated, so a walking NPC slides
  in an idle animation. Owner: S10-T02.
- **Ownership and inventory changes are not replicated:** an item an NPC drops
  stays in its hands on clients (S08). NPC death is not replicated (S06-T05).
- **Crows** are smoothed between the server's 6-m steps. **Distant NPCs**
  follow the server's scheduling steps, carried on for at most 500 ms.
- **Physics objects on a client** are put to sleep at each state; the local
  actor can still nudge them between states (presentation only).
- The snapshot set is capped at 256 entities with no per-player interest
  radius beyond the online set; there is no bandwidth ceiling other than the
  part budget. One host, loopback, simulated impairment only (L16).
- **Single-player start:** two capture runs timed out and one took 3× longer
  than capture-31, cause unknown; it needs a recheck in the next task's
  regressions.
- **Movement program flakiness:** Fanat's following makes the still legs fail
  intermittently; the S06-T03 harness should account for nearby NPCs.
