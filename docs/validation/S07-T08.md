## Where the time went (timing-1, campaign-234, server alone, one actor)

`S07-T08 save-timing` lines in the engine log and `checkpoint-published`
in the co-op log, seven periodic generations:

| Phase (simulation thread before this task) | ms |
|---|---|
| Lua `CALifeStorageManager_before_save` (marshal + the `.scoc` write) | 4–7 |
| World serialization (`header/time/spawns/objects/registry → CMemoryWriter`, 5.9 MB, 26,2xx objects) | 31–58 |

Only the first two read the live world; everything else is file work on
bytes the simulation no longer touches.

## Deliverable

- **Worker thread** (`CoopPersistence`): `begin_publish(job)` hands the
  serialized world and the take's metadata to one worker thread
  (`std::thread`, one job in flight); the worker compresses (LZO's work
  memory is thread-local: `rt_compressor.cpp`), writes the `.scop` in the
  engine's format, builds the supplemental record (the operation index
  under a mutex) and runs the store's steps with no writer
  (`CheckpointStore::publish(…, nullptr)`), then posts a `PublishResult`.
  `poll()` on the simulation thread takes results, switches the journal
  writer to the new generation's file and reports. `wait_idle()` for the
  clean stop. The engine's `CALifeStorageManager::save` hands the
  serialized bytes to `g_alife_save_sink` during a take instead of
  compressing and writing them.
- **The take** (`request_publication`, simulation thread): `track_lifecycle`,
  the native save under the temporary name (Lua `.scoc` + serialization +
  the sink's copy), participants, entities, then the hand-off; logged as
  `checkpoint-taken … sim_ms=` (the whole simulation-thread cost).
- **Journal during a publication**: the writer stays on journal G−1 until
  G is published; the store's recovery reads journals ≥ G−1 and keeps the
  records above `R_G` (store test `async-publication-keeps-previous-journal`,
  st-3/st-4 108/108; contract §6 note).
- **Joins**: a join requests a publication and waits in `WorldPublishing`
  (its lifecycle events queue from the take on) until the worker has
  published it, then streams the generation's files (`resync-requested` →
  `resync-begin … waited_ms=`); a second join while one is in flight waits
  a tick and takes its own; a failed store falls back to the synchronous
  plain save as before.
- **Periodic** publications skip a tick while one is in flight
  (`deferred` counter); **stop** waits for the worker.

```
python3 tools/compare-coop-recovery.py builds/logs/S07-T08/run-rec-4 --out .../compare-recovery.json
```

Recovery unchanged in substance: killed at 271.9 s, recovered generation 9
(revision 1770) with 21 records replayed (money 1508 / 508 = the last
commits), clients rejoined under epoch 2, 173 + 173 operations all
acknowledged, every check passes. Joins waited for their generation
(`resync-begin … waited_ms=361` for the first join under disk contention,
47–67 ms after the restart).

**Simulation-thread cost per checkpoint** (`checkpoint-taken sim_ms`, 18
takes): 45–65 ms in 16, 119 and 129 ms in two (a slow serialization; the
`.scoc` and the copy to the worker are 4–7 ms of it). **Worker time**
(`checkpoint-published worker_ms`): 109–442 ms in the first incarnation
(compress 11, write up to 75, store steps up to 241 with the volume busy),
35–40 ms in the second: none of it on the simulation thread any more.

**What a client sees** (`coop-prediction.csv`, one row per snapshot at
20 Hz, the gap between consecutive snapshot arrivals on c1; the table in the
`stalls` section of `compare-recovery.json` gives the server's own tick gaps):

| Build | Median gap | p99 | Max gap within 1.5 s of a take |
|---|---|---|---|
| rec-3, synchronous (campaign-226) | 50 ms | 87 ms | 183, 283, 367 ms |
| rec-4, worker (campaign-237) | 50 ms | 99 ms | 101, 168, 101, 101, 68, 84 ms |

The stall at a checkpoint fell from up to 370 ms (clearly a hitch) to about
100 ms, the client's interpolation buffer (clients present 100 ms behind
the server clock, so a 100 ms pause is at the edge of visibility); the one
168 ms gap is the 119 ms serialization. The engine's campaign clock logged
2 catch-ups after listening (120 and 160 ms behind) in rec-4's second
incarnation against 1 (140 ms) in rec-3's.

## Limits

- **The world serialization stays on the simulation thread** (31–65 ms
  with 26k objects, twice 119–129 ms in rec-4) plus the scripts' `.scoc`
  write (4–7 ms): they read the live world. A client sees a pause of about
  100 ms at a checkpoint (its interpolation buffer), 168 ms in the slow
  case. Making it smaller means an incremental or copy-on-write
  serialization of the ALife registry (a new task if play tests still show
  a hitch; L34 is updated with these numbers rather than closed).
- The worker and the simulation thread share the machine: the worker's
  compression and writes compete for CPU and disk with the server (and, on
  the test host, with two clients); the campaign clock's catch-ups were not
  more frequent than before.
- One publication in flight at a time: a periodic take due while a join's
  publication is in flight is deferred a tick (`deferred` in the status
  line); a second join while one is in flight waits a tick for its own.
